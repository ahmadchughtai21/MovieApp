# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Film and TV enthusiasts who stream, track, and discuss what they watch. They move between casual discovery (browse, search, pick something tonight), deep viewing (detail page → player), and social logging (diary entries, ratings, reviews, following friends). Context is personal leisure time, often on mobile, often at night.

## Product Purpose

Madflix is a community-driven movie and streaming platform: discover films and shows, stream them, log what you watched, rate and review, and follow friends' activity. Success is a viewer who can go from "what should I watch" to "watching" to "logged and discussed" without friction, and who returns for the social feed as much as the catalog.

## Brand Personality

Sleek, minimal, premium. Quiet confidence of a well-made theater app: dark, restrained, precise. Warmth comes from real film artwork and community activity, not from decoration.

## Anti-references

- Cluttered free-stream site: no ad walls, badge spam, dense link farms, sketchy server lists.
- Sterile corporate SaaS: no flat gray dashboards, generic empty components, zero personality.
- Netflix clone + generic AI: not a rows-of-thumbnails Netflix ripoff, not templated dark-theme AI output (gradient text, glass-everywhere, identical card grids).

## Design Principles

1. Content is the chrome: film artwork, backdrops, and posters carry the color and emotion; UI recedes.
2. One clear next action per screen: Watch, Log, Follow, Browse. Accent color marks only those moments.
3. Social proof sits beside content, never on top of it: ratings, reviews, and friends' activity frame the title.
4. Consistency is an affordance: same button shapes, section rhythm, and nav vocabulary on every page.
5. Density with breathing room: info-rich where it helps decide (meta, facts), generous between sections.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast for body text and controls. Keyboard-reachable nav, player controls, and modals. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Touch targets ≥44px on mobile. Color never the sole state indicator (icons/labels pair with accent states).
